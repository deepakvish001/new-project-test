# 11 — Pricing & Go-To-Market

## 1. The ROI conversation

This is the entire sales pitch, and it fits on one napkin:

```
Customer: ₹5 Cr turnover, ₹1 Cr in receivables, DSO 75 days

DSO 75 → 55 days           = ₹27 lakh working capital freed
Cost of that capital @14%  = ₹3.8 lakh saved per year
Bakaya Growth plan         = ₹72,000 per year
                             ─────────────────────
                             5× return
```

Lead with the ₹27 lakh, not the features. An MSME owner who understands that number does
not need a demo.

## 2. Pricing

| Plan | Price/mo | Active invoices | Includes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Starter** | ₹2,499 | 100 | Ladder rungs 1–4, WhatsApp, payment links, digest, 2 languages |
| **Growth** | ₹5,999 | 500 | + legal pack (43B(h), s.16), all 5 languages, multi-user, Tally connector |
| **Pro** | ₹14,999 | Unlimited | + multi-branch, buyer risk view, priority support, custom templates |
| **Success fee** | 1% of recovered | — | Optional, on invoices 90+ days overdue at signup |

### Pricing decisions and why

- **Annual billing at 2 months free.** Indian MSMEs prefer one payment, and it fixes cash
  flow and churn measurement at once.
- **The success fee is the real upside.** A customer with ₹40 lakh in the 90+ bucket who
  recovers ₹12 lakh pays ₹12,000 on top — and they are *delighted* to, because it is
  money they had written off. Make it opt-in; never make it the headline price.
- **₹2,499 is deliberately low.** Starter exists to get in the door and prove the number.
  Most Starter customers upgrade within 90 days — because that is when they hit an invoice
  that needs the legal pack.
- **The legal pack is the paywall between Starter and Growth.** It is the feature with no
  substitute, and it is what a CA tells them they need.
- **Never discount the list price.** Give an extra month instead. Discounting in a cluster
  gets discussed at the next association meeting, and then it is the price.

## 3. The CA channel — the single most important GTM decision

India has **~4 lakh practising Chartered Accountants**, each serving 50–200 MSME clients.
An MSME owner believes their CA. They do not believe a sales call.

### Why a CA will actually recommend this

1. **43B(h) is already their problem.** They have been telling clients about it since FY
   2023-24 and have had no practical way to act on it. We are the tool they wished existed.
2. **It reduces their own workload.** Chasing a client's receivables data at year-end is
   unpaid, unpleasant work.
3. **₹1,500/client/month recurring**, for a recommendation they would make anyway.
4. **It makes them look good.** A client who recovers ₹8 lakh remembers who suggested it.

### Structure

| Model | Terms |
|---|---|
| Referral | 25% recurring commission, lifetime of the account |
| White-label | ₹1,500/client/mo flat; CA sets their own client price |
| Managed | CA runs collections for clients; ₹999/client/mo + their service fee |

### Execution

- **Start with 10 CAs, not 100.** Pick ones with manufacturing-heavy client books in one
  cluster. Onboard their first client *with them in the room*.
- Build the CA multi-client portal early (Phase 2, week 16) — a CA managing 20 clients
  through 20 separate logins will quietly stop.
- Sponsor ICAI branch study circles in target cities. Cheap, and the audience is exactly
  right.
- **Pay commissions on time, visibly, every month.** The channel runs on trust, and one
  late payment ends a CA relationship permanently.

## 4. Direct GTM — cluster by cluster

**Own one cluster completely before touching the next.** References travel inside a cluster
and almost never between them. Twenty customers in Ludhiana is a defensible position;
twenty customers spread across five cities is nothing.

Sequence: **Ludhiana** (auto parts, cycle parts — dense, WhatsApp-native, severe receivables
pain) → **Rajkot/Surat** → **Pune** (auto components; larger, more sophisticated buyers) →
**Tiruppur/Coimbatore** (needs Tamil, so it comes after week 13).

Channels that work here, in order:

1. **Cluster associations.** Laghu Udyog Bharati, FISME, and the local auto-parts /
   textile associations. A 20-minute slot at a monthly meeting beats 200 cold calls.
2. **Existing-customer referral.** ₹5,000 credit per referral. In a cluster this compounds.
3. **WhatsApp groups.** Every cluster has association groups. Join through a member — never
   spam in; that reputation is unrecoverable.
4. **IndiaMART supplier lists** for cold outreach, but only as top-of-funnel.

Channels that do **not** work for this customer: LinkedIn, Google Ads, content marketing,
product-led self-serve signup. Do not spend money there in year one.

## 5. Onboarding — the real retention battle

A customer who is not live within 48 hours will probably never go live.

```
Day 0   Signup + payment                                    (5 min)
Day 0   Upload Tally Bills Receivable export                (5 min)
Day 0   Review auto-detected buyers, fix merges             (15 min) ← the friction point
Day 0   Pick tone + language, see a preview message         (5 min)
Day 1   Ladder runs on 10 invoices only — a supervised pilot
Day 3   Owner reviews what was sent, approves full rollout
Day 7   First collection attributed → the activation moment
```

**Buyer review is where people drop off.** A 200-buyer list is intimidating. Mitigations:
pre-merge aggressively with good defaults, start with only the top 20 buyers by outstanding
value, and let them approve the rest later.

**Instrument day 7 hard.** "First rupee collected through Bakaya" is the activation metric.
Customers who reach it retain; customers who do not, churn in month two regardless of what
they say on a call.

## 6. Market sizing

| Segment | Count |
|---|---|
| GST-registered businesses in India | 1 crore+ |
| B2B MSMEs selling on credit | ~25–30 lakh |
| Serviceable: ₹1–50 Cr, Tally, cluster-based | ~5 lakh |
| Realistic 3-year target (0.6% of serviceable) | ~3,000 |

```
   500 orgs × ₹6,000 avg  = ₹  30 L MRR  = ₹ 3.6 Cr ARR
 3,000 orgs × ₹6,000 avg  = ₹1.80 Cr MRR = ₹21.6 Cr ARR
```

500 customers is a genuinely good business for a team of six. That is the number to aim at
first — 3,000 is the story for investors, not the plan for year one.

## 7. Competitive position

| Competitor | Segment | Why we don't collide |
|---|---|---|
| Kapittx, Finifi | Mid-market/enterprise AR | ERP integration, ₹1L+/yr, weeks of onboarding, a sales team |
| Recordent | SME credit reporting | Reporting-led, not a collections workflow |
| Tally itself | Everyone | System of record, not a communication layer. We are complementary — say so |
| Generic WhatsApp BSPs | Everyone | Sell a messaging tool; we sell a collections outcome with a legal ladder |

**Our wedge:** self-serve, ₹2,499/month, live in 30 minutes, WhatsApp-first, Hindi-first,
CA-distributed, with the legal ladder nobody else builds. The incumbents cannot come down
here — the economics of their sales model forbid it.

**The real risk is not them.** It is a well-funded Indian SaaS company (Zoho, Razorpay,
Vyapar) shipping this as a feature. Defence: move fast, lock the CA channel early, and get
to the payment-behaviour data moat before they notice the category.
